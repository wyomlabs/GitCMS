**The short answer:** if you build client sites and can work in code, or with a coding agent, the best Squarespace alternative is a code-first site with **GitCMS** as its admin. The client keeps an editor they can use without you, changes can wait for review, and the site is yours instead of a subscription. If you want to stay on a visual builder with more design control, pick **Webflow**. If the client needs a store, bookings, and email in one bill, Squarespace is still hard to beat.

Most designers and small agencies didn't pick Squarespace for its templates. They picked it because the client could log in after launch and change a headline without calling them. People look for Squarespace alternatives when that bundle stops paying for itself: prices went up, the site can't easily leave, and a coding agent now builds a custom site faster than a template can be restyled.

## Why designers leave Squarespace

### Prices went up in 2026

Squarespace raised annual prices by up to 26% in July 2026, and had to apologize for a pricing implementation error along the way. Each site is its own subscription, so an agency with twenty client sites felt it twenty times. Its pricing page now lists three plans, Basic, Core, and Advanced, with prices that vary by region.

### Leaving means rebuilding

Squarespace's own export produces a WordPress XML file, and its help center lists what it leaves out: store, portfolio, album, and other page types, product blocks, video and audio blocks, drafts, every blog after the first, and all style settings and custom CSS. The design never leaves. Rebuilding is the migration plan.

### Edits go live on save

Saving a page publishes it. Blog posts can be set to Needs Review, but page edits have no review step, and the drafts and autosave announced at Squarespace's Circle Day in October 2026 are still in development. That's fine when only you edit the site. It's riskier once clients, teammates, and AI agents all make changes.

### Coding agents made the build cheap

A template you could restyle was the efficient way to deliver when a custom site meant days of development. With Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor, a fast custom site now takes an afternoon. What's left to pay a builder for is the editor your client uses after launch.

## How we ranked them

A disclosure first: I build GitCMS, so treat the ranking as an informed opinion. These four things decide whether a Squarespace replacement works for a designer who hands sites to clients:

1. **Clients edit without you.** The reason you chose Squarespace. A replacement has to keep it.
2. **Review before publish.** Client edits and AI drafts should be able to wait for approval.
3. **The site stays yours.** Code and content you can move, instead of a subscription that holds the design.
4. **It works with agents.** Coding agents build the site, and content agents draft for it.

GitCMS is the CMS for code-first and AI-built websites. It gives your team, your clients, and AI agents one safe place to edit, review, and publish website changes, while everything stays in your site's repository.

It's the Squarespace editor without the Squarespace site. You or your coding agent build the site in code, and it deploys on Vercel, Netlify, or Cloudflare. You invite the client to their site, they sign in with Google, and they edit pages, posts, menus, and footers in a [visual editor](/features/visual-editor/). They never see components or styles, the same boundary Squarespace's Website editor role draws. With [review before publish](/features/editorial-workflow/), changes can wait for your approval.

## When to stay on Squarespace

Stay if the client sells products, takes appointments, or runs email campaigns, and wants it all in one bill. Commerce, scheduling, and email are where Squarespace is genuinely better than a code-first site.

Stay if the owner builds and restyles the site alone and never wants a developer involved.

And stay if the site works and the price still fits. A small site on Basic isn't worth a rebuild.

## Moving a Squarespace site to code

1. **Export what Squarespace can.** Use its WordPress XML export for basic pages and the main blog, then save images and anything else it leaves out, such as product pages and extra blogs, by hand.
2. **Rebuild the design with a coding agent.** Give Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor screenshots of the live site and the exported content, and have it rebuild the site in Astro or Next.js. Keep every URL, and add redirects for any that change.
3. **Convert the content.** Turn the XML into Markdown files with the [convert WordPress posts to Markdown prompt](/prompts/convert-wordpress-posts-to-markdown/); Squarespace's export uses the same format.
4. **Make it client-editable.** Move headlines, hours, and contact details into content files with the [make a site client-editable prompt](/prompts/make-site-client-editable/).
5. **Hand it back.** Connect the repository to GitCMS and invite the client. The [Claude Code client handoff guide](/blog/hand-off-claude-code-website-to-client/) covers the rest.

## FAQ

### What is the best Squarespace alternative for designers?

For designers who build in code or with a coding agent, a code-first site with GitCMS as the client's editor. For designers who want to stay visual with more control, Webflow.

### Can I export my Squarespace site?

Partly. Squarespace exports basic pages and one blog as a WordPress XML file. Styles, custom CSS, store pages, and other page types stay behind, so the design has to be rebuilt.

### Can a client edit a site built with Claude Code like they edit Squarespace?

Yes, once the editable text lives in content files. GitCMS then gives the client a visual editor, with Google sign-in and no access to the design.
