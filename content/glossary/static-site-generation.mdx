## How it works

During the build, the framework runs your page code once per page, fetches content (often from Markdown files), and writes HTML files. Deploying publishes those files to a CDN.

## Where you'll see it

- **Astro** pre-renders pages by default
- **Next.js** statically renders pages that don't use request-time data
- **Hugo, Eleventy, Jekyll** are built entirely around it

## Strengths and limits

SSG gives the fastest page loads and the simplest hosting. Its limit is freshness: content changes need a rebuild, and very large sites can take a while to build.

## SSG and a CMS

With content in the repository, publishing a change means committing it. Your host sees the commit, rebuilds, and the new page is live a minute or two later. Compare [SSG vs SSR](/glossary/ssg-vs-ssr).
