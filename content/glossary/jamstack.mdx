## The idea

The name stood for JavaScript, APIs, and Markup. Instead of rendering every page on a server per request, you build pages ahead of time, deploy them to a CDN, and call APIs from the browser for anything dynamic. Netlify popularized the term around 2016.

## Where it went

The ideas won so thoroughly that the label faded. Modern frameworks like Astro and Next.js mix pre-rendering, server rendering, and edge rendering per page, so "Jamstack" stopped being a distinct architecture.

## What's left

The lasting parts are the ones code-first websites rely on: content and code in Git, sites built on every commit, and pages served fast from the edge. A [Git-based CMS](/glossary/git-based-cms) is the editing layer that grew out of that world.
