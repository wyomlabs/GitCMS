## Typical stages

Most workflows follow the same path: **idea → draft → review → published**. Each stage has an owner. Writers draft, reviewers comment and approve, and someone with publish rights makes it live.

## When you need one

- More than one person edits the site
- Clients or marketers make changes a developer is accountable for
- AI agents edit content, especially in bulk
- You need a record of who approved what

A solo editor can skip it and publish directly. Everyone else benefits from one.

## Why it matters for code-first websites

Developers already have an approval workflow for code: branches and pull requests. Marketers and clients don't use it, so content changes on a code-first site often skip review entirely. A content approval workflow gives non-developers the same safety in their own vocabulary.

## In GitCMS

Sites can use **review before publish**: changes become drafts in a content task, reviewers see inline changes and comments, approvals are tied to a version, and agents can draft and submit for review but not publish. Sites that don't need approval can publish directly. See the [editorial workflow docs](/docs/features/editorial-workflow).
